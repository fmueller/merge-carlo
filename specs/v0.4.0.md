# merge-carlo v0.4.0

## Status and scope

Draft, not active. The active v0.1.0 specification remains the release boundary.
v0.2.0 defines the trunk-based integration core, the merge queue, and a
delivery pipeline whose speed and reliability are aggregate elapsed-time and
failure assumptions. v0.3.0 adds features, activation, further review policies,
follow-up work, and workflow profiles. This specification adds the operational
detail those versions leave out: shared CI and deployment capacity, ordered
deployment environments, trunk breakage, rollback, and staged rollouts. It must
not be implemented by silently changing v0.1.0, v0.2.0, or v0.3.0 semantics.

The extension answers one systems question:

> Under declared CI capacity, deployment capacity, trunk-stability, and
> rollback assumptions, how do integration policies and merge-queue choices
> compare once contention for runners and the cost of a broken trunk or a bad
> deployment are represented?

Results remain conditional simulated outcomes, not causal evidence, productivity
measurement, an availability prediction, or a safety certification.

## Goals

- Represent shared CI and deployment runner capacity, so build wait is
  separated from build execution.
- Represent trunk breakage and its effect on later work, so comparisons between
  merge-queue and no-queue scenarios can show the queue's benefit as well as
  its cost.
- Represent post-deployment failure, detection, and rollback as operational
  events.
- Represent ordered deployment environments and staged rollouts.
- Preserve merge-carlo's provenance, reproducibility, censoring, and uncertainty
  rules.

## Model boundary

The v0.2.0 and v0.3.0 boundaries still apply. The model begins with latent work
increments, performs no Git write and no deployment, and does not simulate
coding time, task selection, or developer WIP.

A trunk breakage, a failed deployment, and a rollback are workflow and
operational events. None of them is a defect label, an incident severity, or a
measure of developer performance. The model does not predict production
availability, user impact, or security outcomes.

## Requirements

### Shared CI capacity

A scenario may declare one or more runner pools with explicit duty calendars
and concurrency. Pull-request verification, merge-queue group builds, trunk
builds, and deployment runs each declare which pool they use. Work that needs a
runner waits in a deterministic queue ordered by `(requested_at, job_kind,
job_id, attempt)`. Execution time is drawn from a declared distribution, and
runner wait and execution time are recorded separately.

When no pool is declared, the v0.2.0 aggregate elapsed-time semantics apply
unchanged. A declared pool replaces that aggregate time for the jobs that use
it; it is never added on top. Runner utilization is reported per pool and never
exceeds one.

### Deployment environments and capacity

A deployment pipeline may declare an ordered list of environments, such as
staging followed by production. Each environment has its own run duration,
failure assumptions, runner pool, and promotion rule. Promotion rules are
closed: `automatic` after a successful run, `scheduled`, or `manual_delay` with
a bounded declared delay. A change reaches production only after it passes
every earlier environment. The v0.2.0 single-environment pipeline is the
special case with one production environment.

### Trunk breakage

Under `direct_verification`, a batch verified against a stale base may break
trunk after it integrates. The breakage probability is a declared assumption or
a bounded response curve over declared inputs, such as the number of trunk
integrations since the batch's verified base. No universal relationship is
built into the product. Breakage draws are keyed by replication, increment,
attempt, and event purpose, never by batch composition or scenario identifier.

Under `merge_queue`, a group is verified against its queue predecessors, so
stale-base breakage does not apply. A scenario may still declare a residual
breakage probability, for example for nondeterministic tests. The model must
not assume that a merge queue prevents every breakage.

While trunk is broken, a closed policy decides the effect on later work:

- `block_integration`: no further batch integrates until trunk is repaired; or
- `fail_verification`: later verifications against the broken trunk fail and
  retry after repair.

Either policy blocks deployment of the breaking change and everything after it.
Repair is a closed choice of `fix_forward` or `revert`, each with a declared
elapsed delay. A revert returns the breaking batch to its integration policy
for rework, and its increments stay conserved. Repair delay is elapsed time,
not modeled coding effort.

### Rollback

A deployed change may carry a declared post-deployment failure probability and
a detection delay. When a failure is detected, a closed policy applies:
`rollback`, which restores the previously deployed state after a declared
delay, or `fix_forward`, which waits a declared delay and deploys again. A
rolled-back increment returns to the pipeline after its fix delay. Rollback
does not undo integration, and it never revives a terminal state other than
the deployment state it reverses. Rollback and detection unfinished at the
horizon are censored.

### Staged rollouts

A production environment may declare a staged rollout with ordered exposure
stages and a hold duration for each. A post-deployment failure detected during
a stage aborts the rollout and applies the rollback policy. Exposure stages are
declared fractions of the rollout, not predictions of the number of affected
users. A staged rollout is still deployment, not v0.3.0 feature activation.

### Metrics

Reports add, each with its denominator, eligibility, censoring, and undefined
reason:

- runner wait and execution time per job kind, and utilization per pool;
- breakages per integrated batch, time trunk spends broken, and integrations or
  verifications blocked or failed by a broken trunk;
- repairs by kind and time to repair;
- time per environment and promotion wait;
- post-deployment failures, rollbacks, time to restore the previous state, and
  aborted rollout stages; and
- work censored at the horizon in each state.

The trade-off vector gains runner wait, trunk-broken time, and rollback cost.
Reports must not label any of these as DORA metrics or compare them with
external benchmarks.

### Data and calibration

Check-run creation, start, and completion times from v0.2.0 CI Enrichment split
observed CI time into runner wait and execution. When a start time is missing,
runner capacity for that job kind is `unavailable`, and the v0.2.0 aggregate
semantics stay in use. Observed wait must never be sampled as execution time
with a simulated runner queue added on top.

Deployment statuses per declared environment supply environment timing,
failures, and rollbacks where the source records them. A revert or rollback is
recognized only from explicit, collected linkage, never from commit-message
text or other weak heuristics. A repository without that evidence records the
dimension as `unavailable`. Breakage observations remain operational signals
and are never promoted to escaped-defect labels under the v0.2.0 quality tiers.

## Candidate scenario suite

1. one runner pool shared by pull-request verification, queue builds, and
   deployment, compared with separate pools;
2. direct-to-trunk integration with declared stale-base breakage, compared with
   the same increments through a merge queue;
3. `block_integration` compared with `fail_verification` while trunk is broken;
4. `fix_forward` compared with `revert` for trunk repair;
5. staging-then-production promotion; and
6. a staged rollout aborted by a post-deployment failure and rolled back.

Acceptance rests on hand-calculated fixtures. Two verifications requested at
time zero on a single always-available runner with a constant 600-second
execution complete at 600 and 1,200; with two runners, both complete at 600.
Further fixtures cover a breakage that blocks a later integration until repair,
a revert that conserves the reverted increments, and a rollback that restores
the previous deployed state.

## Verification requirements

In addition to the repository's normal checks and the v0.2.0 and v0.3.0
verification requirements, the implementation must prove:

- runner utilization never exceeds one, and wait plus execution equals each
  job's elapsed time;
- without a declared pool, results match v0.2.0 aggregate semantics exactly;
- breakage draws for a shared increment are identical across packaging
  policies and gates;
- no change reaches production before passing every earlier environment;
- a revert or rollback conserves increments and never undoes integration;
- horizon censoring does not convert unfinished repair, rollback, or rollout
  into success; and
- reports render from saved artifacts without rerunning simulation.

## Recommendation about LLM support

None, unchanged from earlier versions.

## Explicitly excluded

- infrastructure topology, autoscaling, and cloud cost;
- incident management, on-call load, and paging;
- production availability, user-impact, or revenue predictions;
- defect, security, or severity classification of breakages and failures;
- inferring reverts or rollbacks from commit messages; and
- any GitHub write, deployment action, or enforcement of a repository policy.
