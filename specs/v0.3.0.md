# merge-carlo v0.3.0

## Status and scope

Draft, not active. The active v0.1.0 specification remains the release boundary;
v0.2.0 contains unrelated deferred work. This specification defines a future
workflow-model extension inspired by the non-blocking continuous review
case study. It must not be implemented by silently changing v0.1.0 semantics.

The extension answers a narrower systems question:

> Under declared review-capacity and remediation assumptions, does moving human
> review after verified delivery reduce delivery delay while keeping review debt
> and follow-up work bounded?

Results remain conditional simulated outcomes, not causal evidence, productivity
measurement, or a safety certification.

## Goals

- Distinguish delivery flow from human-review feedback flow.
- Represent continuous post-delivery reviews without treating them as skipped
  reviews or reopening delivered work.
- Compare blocking, continuous, batched, and mixed audited/non-audited workflows
  under the same declared demand and capacity assumptions.
- Make review debt and follow-up work visible instead of hiding them behind a
  faster merge count.
- Preserve merge-carlo's provenance, reproducibility, censoring, and uncertainty
  rules.

## Model boundary

The system begins with a work item that has passed the modeled automated
verification entry point. A delivery is the point at which that work reaches
mainline in the abstraction; the model does not perform a Git write. Human
review is a separate activity that may occur before or after delivery.

The model does not ingest source code, judge design quality, detect defects,
estimate AI-written code, infer productivity, or predict security outcomes.
“Review finding” and “requested change” are workflow observations or assumptions,
not defect labels.

## Requirements

### Workflow policies

The typed workflow contract must support at least:

- `blocking_review`: verification, human review, then delivery;
- `continuous_post_delivery_review`: verification, delivery, then a required
  human review item; and
- `mixed_audit_policy`: audited work uses blocking review while explicitly
  non-audited work uses the selected non-blocking policy.

The existing hypothetical review bypass remains a separate policy. It means that
no human review is scheduled for eligible work and must not be presented as
continuous post-delivery review.

Delivery must remain gated by the declared automated verification contract.
Non-blocking review must never be described as evidence that delivered code is
safe or defect-free.

### Delivery and review state

The domain must use separate lifecycle records or an equivalent typed design:

- a delivery record reaches a terminal delivered, closed, or censored state;
- a post-delivery review item records queue entry, review start, completion,
  reviewer, and censoring;
- a completed review may record requested changes or other declared findings;
- a finding may create a follow-up work item without reviving or undoing the
  original delivery; and
- every record has explicit provenance for observed, derived, proxy, assumed, or
  synthetic values.

Terminal-state invariants must prevent a post-delivery finding, abandonment
deadline, or horizon event from reviving a delivered item.

### Review scheduling and cadence

Post-delivery review items must consume the same explicit active-review capacity
accounting as other reviews: duty calendars, absences, no self-review, stable
ordering, interruption handling, and active-versus-off-duty time separation.

The scenario contract must support at least:

- continuous queueing after delivery; and
- a declared daily or periodic review batch with bounded review capacity.

Queue ordering, simultaneous-event precedence, horizon censoring, and review
service assumptions must be deterministic and documented. A review item that is
unfinished at the horizon is review debt, not a successful review and not a
failed delivery.

The case-study practice of reviewing before starting new work is out of scope
unless a later model extension adds work initiation, coding capacity, and WIP.
The simulator must not claim to measure that behavior from ready-for-review
arrivals alone.

### Follow-up work

The model must allow a declared finding/requested-change probability and an
explicit follow-up delay or work assumption. Follow-up work must be visible in
the output and must not be silently counted as a defect, coding productivity, or
additional review service.

If follow-up consumes a modeled capacity, that capacity and its provenance must
be declared. If it is not modeled, the report must say that remediation is
represented only as a bounded workflow delay or an unresolved assumption.

### Metrics

Reports must distinguish at least:

- ready-to-delivery latency;
- delivery-to-review-start and delivery-to-review-completion latency;
- review queue peak, time-average length, and review-debt count at the horizon;
- review-debt age or bounded age quantiles;
- reviewer active utilization and declared capacity;
- delivered work still awaiting review;
- completed reviews with requested changes/findings;
- follow-up work created, completed, and unresolved; and
- audited work delivered only after its required blocking review.

All metrics require explicit denominators, eligibility, censoring, and undefined
reasons. The report must never replace missing review capacity with zero review
latency or treat review-debt accumulation as a favorable delivery result.

Paired scenario comparisons must reuse shared demand and latent draws where the
work item is common. Any apparent improvement must be labeled conditional on the
workflow, capacity, remediation, and audit assumptions.

### Data and calibration

The input contract must support explicit delivery and post-delivery review
events, including review-to-delivery ordering and follow-up linkage where
available. Direct-mainline commits, feature grouping, and review-tool metadata
must be represented as unavailable or partial when the source cannot establish
them; they must not be reconstructed from unreliable text heuristics.

Active review effort remains assumed or explicitly supplied. Observed elapsed
delivery or feedback delay must not be sampled as active service and then have a
simulated queue added on top.

Historical replay may validate descriptive timing and volume distributions. It
must not claim that non-blocking review caused better quality, faster delivery,
or higher productivity. Insufficient evidence remains an explicit outcome.

### Reporting and interpretation

The report must present the workflow as a trade-off between delivery delay,
feedback delay, review debt, capacity utilization, and follow-up work. It must
include a visible limitations section stating that the model does not establish
defect escape, security risk, policy safety, business value, or individual
performance.

The report may say that a scenario produces faster modeled delivery under its
assumptions. It may not declare the scenario universally superior, safe, or
causally effective. Reversals across assumption sets must be reported, with a
measurement recommendation rather than a policy ranking.

## Candidate scenario suite

The first complete implementation should exercise:

1. the existing blocking-review baseline;
2. continuous post-delivery review;
3. a daily review batch;
4. reviewer absence and additional demand under both policies;
5. a mixed audited/non-audited policy; and
6. immediate-priority follow-up for review findings.

Synthetic fixtures must include no available reviewer, review capacity that
drains accumulated debt, a finding after delivery, and an audited item that
cannot bypass its blocking review.

## Verification requirements

In addition to the repository's normal lint, type, test, and mutation checks,
the implementation must prove:

- delivery can complete while a post-delivery review waits;
- a post-delivery review cannot alter the terminal delivery state;
- review-debt accounting conserves created, completed, and unresolved items;
- audited work never takes the non-blocking path;
- blocking and non-blocking scenarios share equivalent verified demand inputs;
- horizon censoring does not convert unfinished reviews into successes; and
- reports render from saved artifacts without rerunning simulation.

## Recommendation about LLM support

None. The agents remain ordinary stochastic software objects, and the report
remains a deterministic template. LLM personas or generated quality judgments
would add uncontrolled behavior without solving the identification problem.

## Explicitly excluded

- source-code or AST analysis;
- automated defect, security, or design-quality prediction;
- individual productivity scores or developer rankings;
- inferred AI-authorship percentages;
- GitHub writes or enforcement of a repository policy;
- a full coding-time, task-value, or pair-programming simulator;
- a claim that non-blocking review is appropriate for regulated work; and
- automatic policy selection or optimization against historical outcomes.
