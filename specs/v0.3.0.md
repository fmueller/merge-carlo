# merge-carlo v0.3.0

## Status and scope

Draft, not active. The active v0.1.0 specification remains the release boundary;
v0.2.0 contains unrelated deferred work. This specification defines a future
workflow-model extension for comparing trunk-based integration with longer-lived
pull-request branches and for testing non-blocking continuous review. It must
not be implemented by silently changing v0.1.0 semantics.

The extension answers two related systems questions:

> Under declared work-packaging, verification, integration-friction, review, and
> release assumptions, how do direct-to-trunk and short-lived-branch workflows
> compare with longer-lived pull-request branches?

> Under declared review-capacity and remediation assumptions, does moving human
> review after verified trunk integration reduce integration delay while keeping
> review debt and follow-up work bounded?

Results remain conditional simulated outcomes, not causal evidence, productivity
measurement, or a safety certification.

## Goals

- Distinguish delivery flow from human-review feedback flow.
- Represent direct-to-trunk, short-lived pull-request, and longer-lived
  pull-request integration as distinct policies rather than treating
  trunk-based development as synonymous with post-integration review.
- Preserve common latent work across paired scenarios while allowing policies
  to package that work into different commits and integration batches.
- Separate trunk integration, production deployment, and user-visible feature
  activation.
- Represent continuous post-integration reviews without treating them as skipped
  reviews or reopening integrated work.
- Compare blocking, continuous, batched, and mixed audited/non-audited workflows
  under the same declared demand and capacity assumptions.
- Make review debt and follow-up work visible instead of hiding them behind a
  faster integration count.
- Make fit to professional repository workflow policies explicit instead of
  treating unsupported approval, routing, queue, or backlog semantics as absent.
- Preserve merge-carlo's provenance, reproducibility, censoring, and uncertainty
  rules.

## Model boundary

The first trunk-based slice begins with latent work increments ready to enter
the modeled integration workflow. Coding time, task selection, developer WIP,
and the process that produced those increments remain outside the boundary.
Branch lifetime, packaging cadence, and batch size are declared scenario inputs,
not consequences of simulated coding behavior.

Integration is the point at which an integration batch reaches trunk in the
abstraction; the model does not perform a Git write. Deployment to production
and user-visible feature activation are separate optional events. A policy may
make deployment immediate at integration, but the model must not equate those
events silently. Human review is another separate activity that may occur before
or after integration.

The model does not ingest source code, judge design quality, detect defects,
estimate AI-written code, infer productivity, or predict security outcomes.
“Review finding” and “requested change” are workflow observations or assumptions,
not defect labels.

## Requirements

### Work, feature, and integration units

The domain must distinguish:

- a latent work increment, the smallest conserved unit shared by paired
  scenarios;
- a feature, containing one or more latent increments;
- an integration batch, containing one or more increments packaged for one
  attempt to reach trunk; and
- the commits or pull request representing that batch where the input evidence
  can establish them.

A feature may consist of one commit, multiple commits in one integration batch,
or multiple batches integrated and deployed over time. A feature may remain
inactive after deployment and become user-visible only through a later feature-
activation event. Activation eligibility requires every increment declared as
necessary for that feature to be deployed; the model must not expose each
incomplete increment as an independently delivered feature.

Paired scenario comparisons must begin with the same latent increments and
feature membership. Integration policies may group those increments differently,
but must conserve increment identity and count. A scenario may not gain an
apparent flow advantage by dropping, duplicating, or regenerating underlying
work.

### Integration policies

The typed integration-policy contract must support at least:

- `direct_to_trunk`, in which each eligible batch integrates without a pull-
  request branch after satisfying its declared gates;
- `short_lived_pull_request`, in which small batches use a bounded-lifetime
  branch and pull request; and
- `long_lived_pull_request`, the comparison policy, in which larger or older
  batches integrate through a pull request.

Batch-size and branch-lifetime distributions, maximums, and packaging cadence
must be explicit, versioned scenario inputs with provenance. “Short-lived” and
“long-lived” are not hard-coded universal durations. Presets may provide
documented defaults, but reports must render their resolved values.

The first slice must use closed, typed packaging rules rather than dynamically
loaded code. It must support one increment per batch and a bounded rule that
closes a batch at a configured increment count, elapsed age, or cadence boundary,
whichever applies first. Increment selection is stable by `(ready_at,
increment_id)`, and a rule may not inspect future arrivals. Historical replay
may preserve an observed grouping when its membership is explicit and complete.

Integration policy and human-review policy are separate dimensions. The product
may provide realistic named presets, but its scenario contract must also permit
factorial comparisons where combinations are semantically valid. Invalid
combinations must be rejected rather than silently rewritten.

### Verification and integration friction

Every integration batch must satisfy its declared automated verification gate.
The first trunk-based slice must support bounded verification failures and
retries plus conflict or rebase delay. These are workflow events, not evidence
of a defect or a measure of developer productivity.

Scenarios may directly declare provenance-tagged friction distributions. They
may alternatively declare bounded response curves relating friction to inputs
such as branch age, batch size, or intervening trunk integrations. No universal
monotonic relationship is built into the product. A fitted historical response
curve is preferred when its evidence is sufficiently complete, validated on a
temporal holdout, and used within its observed support; otherwise the model must
fall back to explicit assumptions and sensitivity analysis. Extrapolated
counterfactuals must be labeled as such.

Observed elapsed branch, CI, or pull-request duration must not be treated as
active conflict-resolution or verification effort. Missing conflict events,
rebases, retry causes, or direct-to-trunk history remain unavailable rather than
being reconstructed from commit messages or weak merge-state heuristics.

### Workflow policies

The typed human-review contract must support at least:

- `blocking_review`: verification, human review, then integration;
- `continuous_post_integration_review`: verification, integration, then a
  required human review item;
- `batched_post_integration_review`: integration followed by review in a
  declared daily or periodic batch;
- `paired_pre_integration_review`: declared concurrent human collaboration that
  satisfies the review gate while consuming explicit paired-review capacity;
  and
- `mixed_audit_policy`: audited work uses blocking review while explicitly
  non-audited work uses the selected non-blocking policy.

The existing hypothetical review bypass remains a separate policy. It means that
no human review is scheduled for eligible work and must not be presented as
continuous post-integration review.

Integration must remain gated by the declared automated verification contract.
The paired policy does not simulate coding collaboration, pair effectiveness, or
individual behavior. Non-blocking review must never be described as evidence
that integrated or deployed code is safe or defect-free.

### Deployment and feature activation

The first trunk-based slice must provide closed, typed deployment policies for
immediate deployment, deployment in a declared schedule or batch, and a bounded
stochastic deployment delay. It does not simulate deployment-runner capacity,
infrastructure topology, rollout failures, or rollback.

Feature activation must be a separate optional event. An eligible feature may
activate immediately after all required increments are deployed, at a declared
scheduled time, or after a bounded delay representing a later feature-flag flip.
A deployed but inactive feature remains distinct from both undeployed work and
user-visible work. Deployment or activation unfinished at the horizon is
censored, never converted into success or a zero delay.

### Integration, delivery, activation, and review state

The domain must use separate lifecycle records or an equivalent typed design:

- an integration batch reaches a terminal integrated, closed, or censored state;
- deployment and feature-activation records preserve their own eligibility,
  completion, and censoring state;
- a post-integration review item records queue entry, review start, completion,
  reviewer, and censoring;
- a completed review may record requested changes or other declared findings;
- a finding may create a follow-up work item without reviving or undoing the
  original integration, deployment, or activation; and
- every record has explicit provenance for observed, derived, proxy, assumed, or
  synthetic values.

Terminal-state invariants must prevent a post-integration finding, abandonment
deadline, or horizon event from reviving an integrated item.

### Review scheduling and cadence

Post-integration review items must consume the same explicit active-review capacity
accounting as other reviews: duty calendars, absences, no self-review, stable
ordering, interruption handling, and active-versus-off-duty time separation.

The scenario contract must support at least:

- continuous queueing after integration; and
- a declared daily or periodic review batch with bounded review capacity.

Queue ordering, simultaneous-event precedence, horizon censoring, and review
service assumptions must be deterministic and documented. A review item that is
unfinished at the horizon is review debt, not a successful review and not a
failed integration or delivery.

The case-study practice of reviewing before starting new work is out of scope
unless a later model extension adds work initiation, coding capacity, and WIP.
The simulator must not claim to measure that behavior from ready-to-integrate
increments alone.

### Follow-up work

The model must allow a declared finding/requested-change probability and an
explicit follow-up delay or work assumption. Follow-up work must be visible in
the output and must not be silently counted as a defect, coding productivity, or
additional review service.

If follow-up consumes a modeled capacity, that capacity and its provenance must
be declared. If it is not modeled, the report must say that remediation is
represented only as a bounded workflow delay or an unresolved assumption.

### Metrics

Reports must distinguish at least:

- ready-to-integration, ready-to-deployment, and ready-to-feature-activation
  latency;
- integration-to-deployment and deployment-to-activation latency;
- integration-to-review-start and integration-to-review-completion latency;
- integration-batch size and age, branch lifetime, verification attempts and
  retries, and declared conflict or rebase delay;
- review queue peak, time-average length, and review-debt count at the horizon;
- review-debt age or bounded age quantiles;
- reviewer active utilization and declared capacity;
- integrated and deployed work still awaiting review;
- deployed features still awaiting activation;
- completed reviews with requested changes/findings;
- follow-up work created, completed, and unresolved; and
- audited work integrated only after its required blocking review.

All metrics require explicit denominators, eligibility, censoring, and undefined
reasons. The report must never replace missing review capacity with zero review
latency or treat review-debt accumulation as a favorable integration result.

The report must present integration speed, deployment speed, feature-exposure
speed, review debt, verification rework, and conflict cost as a trade-off vector.
No one metric is the default winner or an optimization objective.

Paired scenario comparisons must reuse shared demand and latent draws where the
work item is common. Any apparent improvement must be labeled conditional on the
workflow, capacity, remediation, and audit assumptions.

### Data and calibration

The input contract must support explicit branch, commit, pull-request,
integration, deployment, feature-activation, and post-integration review events,
including event ordering and follow-up linkage where available. Direct-mainline
commits, feature grouping, conflict work, deployment, activation, and review-tool
metadata must each carry collection completeness. They must be represented as
unavailable or partial when the source cannot establish them and must not be
reconstructed from unreliable text heuristics.

Active review effort remains assumed or explicitly supplied. Observed elapsed
integration, delivery, or feedback delay must not be sampled as active service
and then have a simulated queue added on top.

Historical replay may validate descriptive timing and volume distributions. A
later version may use validated historical relationships to construct explicit
counterfactual assumptions, while preserving observed-support limits and
sensitivity ranges. Replay or fitting must not claim that trunk integration or
non-blocking review caused better quality, faster delivery, or higher
productivity. Insufficient evidence remains an explicit outcome.

### Professional workflow profiles

The input contract must let a repository declare workflow dimensions that affect
integration, delivery, and review semantics: integration strategy, branch and
batch policy, required approval count, reviewer routing or ownership pools,
merge gating and queue behavior, deployment and feature-activation policy,
closure-without-integration rules, initial backlog, and audited-work policy.
Each dimension carries a basis of `observed`, `derived`, `proxy`, `assumed`, or
`synthetic`, plus a status of `supported`, `partial`, or `unavailable`.

The model and report must not silently collapse a repository with multiple
required approvals, routed reviewers, protected-branch gates, merge queues, or
an initial backlog into the single-reviewer FIFO abstraction. A profile that
uses unsupported dimensions is either rejected for the requested validation or
marked as a structural-fit limitation; it is never presented as a successful
calibration. Initial backlog must be represented or marked unavailable rather
than replaced with an empty start.

The first v0.3 implementation may provide profile declaration, compatibility
diagnostics, and bounded aggregate approximations. Full CODEOWNERS resolution,
arbitrary branch-protection emulation, and repository-specific merge-queue
policies need separately specified contracts and fixtures; hidden heuristics
must not introduce them.

### Reporting and interpretation

The report must present the workflow as a trade-off between integration delay,
delivery and activation delay, feedback delay, review debt, capacity utilization,
verification rework, conflict cost, and follow-up work. It must
include a visible limitations section stating that the model does not establish
defect escape, security risk, policy safety, business value, or individual
performance.

The report may say that a scenario produces faster modeled integration or
delivery under its assumptions. It may not declare trunk-based development or
another scenario universally superior, safe, or causally effective. Reversals
across assumption sets must be reported, with a measurement recommendation
rather than a policy ranking.

## Staged scope

The first trunk-based slice uses declared work-packaging, branch-lifetime,
verification, conflict, deployment, activation, review, and remediation
assumptions. It does not derive branch behavior from developer task execution.

A later specification may extend the boundary to task starts, coding capacity,
developer WIP, commit production, branch aging, the rule to review debt before
starting new work, trunk instability and affected downstream work, deployment-
pipeline capacity, failure, rollback, and fix-forward behavior. It may also
calibrate counterfactual response curves from richer historical evidence. Those
capabilities are not implied by or required for v0.3.

## Candidate scenario suite

The first complete implementation should exercise:

1. a longer-lived pull-request and blocking-review baseline;
2. short-lived pull requests with the same latent increments and blocking
   review;
3. verified direct-to-trunk integration;
4. continuous and daily-batched post-integration review as independent review
   policies;
5. immediate, scheduled, and bounded-delay deployment;
6. a multi-increment feature deployed over several batches and activated later;
7. reviewer absence and additional demand across integration policies;
8. a mixed audited/non-audited policy; and
9. immediate-priority follow-up for review findings.

Synthetic fixtures must include no available reviewer, review capacity that
drains accumulated debt, a finding after integration, and an audited item that
cannot bypass its blocking review. They must also include common latent
increments grouped into different batches, a verification retry, bounded
conflict delay, deployment without activation, and activation only after all
required feature increments are deployed.

## Verification requirements

In addition to the repository's normal lint, type, test, and mutation checks,
the implementation must prove:

- latent increments and feature membership are conserved across paired branch
  strategies;
- direct-to-trunk, short-lived pull-request, and longer-lived pull-request
  scenarios resolve distinct integration policies without changing shared work;
- an integration can complete while a post-integration review waits;
- a post-integration review cannot alter terminal integration, deployment, or
  activation state;
- deployment and activation remain distinct unless an immediate policy is
  explicitly selected;
- a multi-increment feature cannot activate before all required increments are
  deployed;
- review-debt accounting conserves created, completed, and unresolved items;
- audited work never takes the non-blocking path;
- blocking and non-blocking scenarios share equivalent latent demand inputs;
- unsupported historical fitting and extrapolation are rejected or visibly
  qualified;
- horizon censoring does not convert unfinished reviews into successes; and
- reports render from saved artifacts without rerunning simulation.

## Recommendation about LLM support

None. The agents remain ordinary stochastic software objects, and the report
remains a deterministic template. LLM personas or generated quality judgments
would add uncontrolled behavior without solving the identification problem.

## Explicitly excluded

- source-code or AST analysis;
- automated defect, security, or design-quality prediction;
- individual productivity scores or developer rankings;
- inferred AI-authorship percentages;
- GitHub writes or enforcement of a repository policy;
- a full coding-time, task-value, developer-WIP, or pair-programming simulator;
- deployment-runner capacity, infrastructure topology, rollout failure,
  rollback, trunk-breakage propagation, or downstream-work disruption in the
  first trunk-based slice;
- a claim that non-blocking review is appropriate for regulated work; and
- automatic policy selection or optimization against historical outcomes.
