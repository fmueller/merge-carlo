# merge-carlo v0.3.0

## Status and scope

Draft, not active. The active v0.1.0 specification remains the release boundary.
v0.2.0 defines the trunk-based integration core: latent increments,
integration batches, the three integration policies, the direct-verification
and merge-queue gates, verification and conflict friction, blocking and
continuous post-integration review, and the delivery pipeline to production.
This specification extends that core with features and feature activation,
further non-blocking review policies, follow-up work, and professional workflow
profiles. Shared CI and deployment capacity, trunk breakage, and rollback are
v0.4.0 scope. It must not be implemented
by silently changing v0.1.0 or v0.2.0 semantics.

The extension answers two related systems questions:

> Under declared integration, delivery-pipeline, and activation assumptions,
> how long does work take to become visible to users under each integration
> policy, and how long do deployed features wait for activation?

> Under declared review-capacity and remediation assumptions, does moving human
> review after verified trunk integration reduce integration delay while keeping
> review debt and follow-up work bounded?

Results remain conditional simulated outcomes, not causal evidence, productivity
measurement, or a safety certification.

## Goals

- Distinguish delivery flow from human-review feedback flow.
- Separate v0.2.0 production deployment from user-visible feature activation.
- Represent features that span several increments and batches.
- Compare blocking, continuous, batched, paired, and mixed audited/non-audited
  review under the same declared demand and capacity assumptions.
- Make follow-up work after post-integration findings visible instead of hiding
  it behind a faster integration count.
- Make fit to professional repository workflow policies explicit instead of
  treating unsupported approval, routing, queue, or backlog semantics as absent.
- Preserve merge-carlo's provenance, reproducibility, censoring, and uncertainty
  rules.

## Model boundary

The v0.2.0 boundary still applies. The model begins with latent work increments
ready to enter the integration workflow. Coding time, task selection, developer
WIP, and the process that produced those increments remain outside it, and the
model performs no Git write.

Production deployment comes from the v0.2.0 delivery pipeline. User-visible
feature activation is a separate optional event after deployment. A policy may
make activation immediate at deployment, but the model must not equate those
events silently. Human review
is another separate activity that may occur before or after integration.

The model does not ingest source code, judge design quality, detect defects,
estimate AI-written code, infer productivity, or predict security outcomes.
"Review finding" and "requested change" are workflow observations or
assumptions, not defect labels.

## Requirements

### Features

A feature contains one or more latent increments. It may consist of one commit,
several commits in one integration batch, or several batches integrated and
deployed over time. A feature may remain inactive after deployment and become
user-visible only through a later activation event. Activation eligibility
requires every increment declared as necessary for that feature to be deployed.
The model must not expose each incomplete increment as an independently
delivered feature.

Paired scenario comparisons begin with the same latent increments and the same
feature membership. The v0.2.0 conservation rules for increments extend to
feature membership.

### Review policies

The typed human-review contract adds to the v0.2.0 `blocking_review` and
`continuous_post_integration_review` policies:

- `batched_post_integration_review`: integration followed by review in a
  declared daily or periodic batch with bounded review capacity;
- `paired_pre_integration_review`: declared concurrent human collaboration that
  satisfies the review gate while consuming explicit paired-review capacity;
  and
- `mixed_audit_policy`: audited work uses blocking review, while explicitly
  non-audited work uses the selected non-blocking policy.

Integration remains gated by the declared automated verification contract. The
paired policy does not simulate coding collaboration, pair effectiveness, or
individual behavior. Non-blocking review must never be described as evidence
that integrated or deployed code is safe or defect-free.

Batched review uses the v0.2.0 review capacity accounting, ordering,
precedence, and censoring rules. A review item unfinished at the horizon is
review debt, not a successful review and not a failed integration or delivery.

The case-study practice of reviewing before starting new work is out of scope
unless a later model extension adds work initiation, coding capacity, and WIP.
The simulator must not claim to measure that behavior from ready-to-integrate
increments alone.

### Feature activation

Deployment uses the v0.2.0 delivery pipeline unchanged. Feature activation is
a separate optional event with a closed, typed policy. An eligible feature may
activate immediately after all its required increments are deployed, at a
declared scheduled time, or after a bounded delay that represents a later
feature-flag flip. A deployed but inactive feature remains distinct from both
undeployed work and user-visible work. Activation unfinished at the horizon is
censored, never converted into success or a zero delay.

### Activation and follow-up state

The v0.2.0 integration, deployment, and review records gain:

- feature-activation records that keep their own eligibility, completion, and
  censoring state; and
- follow-up work items linked to the finding that created them.

A finding may create a follow-up work item without reviving or undoing the
original integration, deployment, or activation. Every record keeps explicit
provenance for observed, derived, proxy, assumed, or synthetic values.

### Follow-up work

The model must allow a declared finding or requested-change probability, and an
explicit follow-up delay or work assumption. Follow-up work must be visible in
the output. It must not be silently counted as a defect, coding productivity, or
additional review service.

If follow-up consumes a modeled capacity, that capacity and its provenance must
be declared. If it is not modeled, the report must say that remediation is
represented only as a bounded workflow delay or an unresolved assumption.

### Metrics

Reports add to the v0.2.0 trunk-based metrics:

- ready-to-feature-activation and deployment-to-activation latency;
- integrated and deployed work still awaiting review;
- deployed features still awaiting activation;
- follow-up work created, completed, and unresolved; and
- audited work integrated only after its required blocking review.

All metrics require explicit denominators, eligibility, censoring, and undefined
reasons. The trade-off vector gains feature-exposure speed and follow-up
work. No one metric is the default winner or an optimization
objective. Any apparent improvement must be labeled conditional on the
workflow, capacity, remediation, and audit assumptions.

### Data and calibration

The input contract adds explicit feature-activation and follow-up linkage
events to the v0.2.0 events. Feature grouping and activation metadata each
carry collection completeness. They must be
represented as unavailable or partial when the source cannot establish them and
must not be reconstructed from unreliable text heuristics.

Observed elapsed integration, delivery, or feedback delay must not be sampled as
active service and then have a simulated queue added on top. A later version may
use validated historical relationships to construct explicit counterfactual
assumptions, while preserving observed-support limits and sensitivity ranges.
Replay or fitting must not claim that trunk integration or non-blocking review
caused better quality, faster delivery, or higher productivity. Insufficient
evidence remains an explicit outcome.

### Professional workflow profiles

The input contract must let a repository declare workflow dimensions that affect
integration, delivery, and review semantics: integration strategy, branch and
batch policy, required approval count, reviewer routing or ownership pools,
merge gating and queue behavior, deployment and feature-activation policy,
closure-without-integration rules, initial backlog, and audited-work policy.
Each dimension carries a basis of `observed`, `derived`, `proxy`, `assumed`, or
`synthetic`, plus a status of `supported`, `partial`, or `unavailable`.

The model and report must not silently collapse a repository with multiple
required approvals, routed reviewers, protected-branch gates, merge queues, or
an initial backlog into the single-reviewer FIFO abstraction. A profile that
uses unsupported dimensions is either rejected for the requested validation or
marked as a structural-fit limitation; it is never presented as a successful
calibration. Initial backlog must be represented or marked unavailable rather
than replaced with an empty start.

The first v0.3 implementation may provide profile declaration, compatibility
diagnostics, and bounded aggregate approximations. A profile's queue dimension
is `supported` when it fits the v0.2.0 merge-queue contract: group size, the
minimum-size wait, build concurrency, and a declared failure-isolation rule.
It is `partial` when it also uses queue features outside that contract, such as
priority jumps, entries that must build alone, per-check required subsets, or
multiple target branches. It is `unavailable` when queue history was not
collected. Full CODEOWNERS resolution, arbitrary branch-protection emulation,
and those repository-specific queue features need separately specified
contracts and fixtures. Hidden heuristics must not introduce them.

### Reporting and interpretation

The report must present the workflow as a trade-off between integration delay,
delivery and activation delay, feedback delay, review debt, capacity utilization,
verification rework, queue build cost, conflict cost, and follow-up work. It
must include a visible limitations section stating that the model does not
establish defect escape, security risk, policy safety, business value, or
individual performance. The v0.2.0 trunk-breakage limitation still applies to
every comparison across integration gates.

The report may say that a scenario produces faster modeled integration or
delivery under its assumptions. It may not declare trunk-based development or
another scenario universally superior, safe, or causally effective. Reversals
across assumption sets must be reported, with a measurement recommendation
rather than a policy ranking.

## Staged scope

This version uses declared activation, review, and remediation assumptions on
top of the v0.2.0 core. It does not derive branch behavior from
developer task execution.

A later specification may extend the boundary to task starts, coding capacity,
developer WIP, commit production, branch aging, and the rule to review debt
before starting new work. It may also calibrate counterfactual response curves
from richer historical evidence. Shared CI and deployment capacity, trunk
breakage and its effect on downstream work, rollback, and staged rollouts are
v0.4.0 scope. None of these capabilities is implied by or required for v0.3.

## Candidate scenario suite

The first complete implementation should exercise:

1. continuous and daily-batched post-integration review as independent review
   policies;
2. immediate, scheduled, and delayed feature activation after deployment;
3. a multi-increment feature deployed over several batches and activated later;
4. a mixed audited/non-audited policy;
5. paired pre-integration review with declared paired-review capacity; and
6. immediate-priority follow-up for review findings.

Synthetic fixtures must include a finding that creates follow-up work after
integration, an audited item that cannot bypass its blocking review, deployment
without activation, and activation only after all required feature increments
are deployed.

## Verification requirements

In addition to the repository's normal lint, type, test, and mutation checks
and the v0.2.0 trunk-based verification requirements, the implementation must
prove:

- feature membership is conserved across paired integration policies;
- a post-integration review or follow-up item cannot alter terminal
  integration, deployment, or activation state;
- deployment and activation remain distinct unless immediate activation is
  explicitly selected;
- a multi-increment feature cannot activate before all required increments are
  deployed;
- follow-up accounting conserves created, completed, and unresolved items;
- audited work never takes the non-blocking path;
- blocking and every non-blocking review scenario share equivalent latent
  demand inputs;
- unsupported profile dimensions are rejected or visibly qualified;
- horizon censoring does not convert unfinished activation or review into
  success; and
- reports render from saved artifacts without rerunning simulation.

## Recommendation about LLM support

None. The agents remain ordinary stochastic software objects, and the report
remains a deterministic template. LLM personas or generated quality judgments
would add uncontrolled behavior without solving the identification problem.

## Explicitly excluded

- source-code or AST analysis;
- automated defect, security, or design-quality prediction;
- individual productivity scores or developer rankings;
- inferred AI-authorship percentages;
- GitHub writes or enforcement of a repository policy;
- a full coding-time, task-value, developer-WIP, or pair-programming simulator;
- CI and deployment runner capacity, rollback, staged rollouts, trunk-breakage
  propagation, and downstream-work disruption, which are v0.4.0 scope;
- infrastructure topology;
- merge-queue features outside the v0.2.0 contract, such as priority jumps,
  entries that must build alone, per-check required subsets, multiple target
  branches, and build-runner capacity;
- a claim that non-blocking review is appropriate for regulated work; and
- automatic policy selection or optimization against historical outcomes.
