# merge-carlo v0.5.0

## Status and scope

Draft, not active. The active v0.2.0 specification is the release boundary.
v0.2.0 defines the quality-outcome data contract and descriptive reporting of
quality signals, and names a latent defect-introduction model, detection,
post-merge escape, and remediation feedback as subsequent milestones. This
specification defines those milestones as an optional layer over the v0.2.0
trunk-based core, the v0.3.0 increment size, stacking, size-based review
eligibility, and follow-up work, and, where declared, the v0.4.0 rollback and
staged rollouts. It must not be implemented by silently changing v0.1.0,
v0.2.0, v0.3.0, or v0.4.0 semantics.

The extension answers one systems question:

> Under declared defect-introduction, detection, and remediation assumptions,
> how do review, packaging, stacking, and size-based eligibility policies trade
> integration and delivery speed against modeled defect escape and remediation
> demand?

Results remain conditional simulated outcomes under declared assumptions. They
are not defect predictions, a measured escape rate, a security assessment,
causal evidence, or a safety certification for any policy or threshold.

## Goals

- Give the speed side of review, packaging, and eligibility comparisons a
  modeled quality cost, so that a policy that skips review cannot look free.
- Hold the latent defects fixed across paired scenarios, so policies differ
  only in what they detect and when.
- Separate detection by verification, pre-integration review,
  post-integration review, and post-deployment observation.
- Let detected defects create remediation work that consumes declared
  capacity and changes later queue, integration, and delivery outcomes.
- Make every defect quantity's sensitivity to its assumptions visible.
- Preserve merge-carlo's provenance, reproducibility, censoring, and uncertainty
  rules.

## Model boundary

The v0.2.0, v0.3.0, and v0.4.0 boundaries still apply. The model begins with
latent work increments, performs no Git write and no deployment, and does not
ingest source code.

A latent defect is a model quantity drawn from declared assumptions. It is
never inferred from a repository, and the model does not detect, classify, or
predict real defects. The v0.2.0 quality tiers remain observations: a requested
change, a failed check, a static-analysis finding, a trunk breakage, a failed
deployment, a revert, or a rollback is never silently promoted to a latent or
escaped defect.

## Requirements

### Latent defect introduction

When the layer is enabled, each increment receives a latent defect count drawn
from a declared, overdispersed count distribution whose exposure is the
increment's v0.3.0 changed-line size. The rate may vary with declared cohort
attributes such as task type or origin cohort, each as a declared, uncertain
assumption, never a universal multiplier. Origin follows the v0.2.0 rules:
unknown stays unknown and is never reassigned. An increment without a known
size cannot receive size-based exposure; a scenario must either declare a
size-free rate for it, with its basis, or exclude it from defect metrics with
its count shown.

Introduction draws are keyed by replication, increment, and defect index,
never by batch, stack, policy, or scenario identifier. Defects belong to
increments, so packaging, stacking, size caps, and eligibility rules cannot
create or remove defects. Any effect of batch size on quality must be declared
as an effect on detection below, not on introduction.

### Defect classes

Each latent defect has one class from a closed, declared set, with a declared
severity label and a basis. A scenario may declare a `security` class only with
an `assumed` or `synthetic` basis. Reports render it as an assumed class and
never as a security-risk estimate, a vulnerability count, or a measured
security outcome. Severity labels are declared assumptions, not incident
severity.

### Detection stages

A latent defect can be detected at most once, at the first stage whose
detection draw succeeds:

1. verification, per verification attempt;
2. pre-integration human review, per review visit;
3. post-integration review, per review item under the v0.2.0 and v0.3.0
   non-blocking policies; and
4. post-deployment observation, after a declared detection delay.

Each stage declares a detection probability per defect class. Review detection
may instead declare a bounded response curve over the reviewed batch's
changed lines or changed files, under the v0.2.0 friction rules: no universal
relationship is built into the product, a fitted curve is used only within its
observed support after temporal holdout validation, and extrapolation is
labeled. Detection draws are keyed by replication, defect, stage, and attempt,
never by batch composition or scenario identifier.

Work that bypasses review, and non-audited work under `mixed_audit_policy`,
skips pre-integration review detection. Verification and later stages still
apply.

### Consistency with workflow probabilities

Enabling the layer must not double-count rework. The v0.1.0 and v0.2.0
verification-failure and requested-change probabilities are split into a
defect-caused component, produced by detection above, and a declared
non-defect component, such as flaky checks or style requests. The two
components are declared separately and never summed with the original
probability. A scenario that declares only the original probabilities runs with
the layer disabled.

### Remediation

A defect detected before integration routes the batch through AUTHOR_RESPONSE
and a new revision under the v0.1.0 rules; the revision removes that defect. A
defect detected after integration creates v0.3.0 follow-up work linked to the
defect, with a declared delay and, where declared, capacity use. A defect
detected after deployment applies the v0.4.0 rollback or fix-forward policy
when the scenario declares one, and otherwise creates follow-up work. Fix
revisions draw no new defects in the first slice, and reports say so.
Remediation never revives a terminal state or undoes an integration.

### Escape and censoring

A defect integrated undetected has escaped to trunk. A defect deployed
undetected has escaped to production. A defect undetected at the horizon is
censored as undetected, never counted as absent. Post-deployment detection uses
a declared observation window, and reports render it with every escape metric.

### Metrics

Reports add, each with its denominator, eligibility, censoring, and undefined
reason:

- modeled defects introduced, per increment and per thousand changed lines;
- modeled defects detected by stage, and the share of introduced defects each
  stage detects;
- modeled defects escaped to trunk and to production, per integrated and per
  deployed increment, overall and by class;
- detection delay from integration and from deployment;
- remediation work created, completed, and unresolved, and the capacity it
  consumed; and
- modeled defects censored as undetected at the horizon.

The v0.2.0 trade-off vector gains modeled escape and remediation demand. The
v0.1.0 report fields `defect_escape_rate`, `security_risk_change`, and
`policy_safety` keep their meaning as measured quantities and stay `null` with
their unsupported reason; modeled quantities use separate, explicitly modeled
names and are never written into those fields.

### Data and calibration

Introduction and detection parameters may be calibrated only from v0.2.0
quality tiers with explicit, collected linkage, within their observation
windows, with uncertainty-aware fitting for sparse and overdispersed counts and
temporal holdout validation. Otherwise they remain declared assumptions. The
observed tiers measure detected outcomes, not introduction, so an observed
rate is never used as an introduction rate without a declared detection model.
Insufficient evidence remains an explicit outcome.

Every report on a defect scenario renders sensitivity over declared ranges for
at least the introduction rate and the review-detection assumption. A
direction that reverses within those ranges is reported as a reversal with a
measurement recommendation, not as a result.

## Candidate scenario suite

1. full blocking review compared with size-based bypass across a sweep of
   `size_threshold` maximums, over the same latent defects;
2. the same feature increments as one large batch, a stack, and independent
   small batches, with constant review detection and with detection that
   declines with batch size;
3. continuous post-integration review compared with blocking review;
4. `mixed_audit_policy` with several audit fractions;
5. an assumed `security` class with a low introduction rate and high severity;
   and
6. post-deployment detection with v0.4.0 rollback compared with follow-up work
   only.

Acceptance rests on hand-calculated fixtures with probabilities of zero or
one. One increment carries one defect; verification never detects it and
review always does. Under blocking review the defect is detected at review and
never escapes. Under bypass the same defect escapes to trunk. Further fixtures
cover a defect censored as undetected at the horizon, a post-integration
detection that creates follow-up work without undoing the integration, and the
same latent defects under two packaging policies.

## Verification requirements

In addition to the repository's normal checks and the v0.2.0 through v0.4.0
verification requirements, the implementation must prove:

- latent defects are identical across packaging, stacking, eligibility, and
  review scenarios that share increments;
- introduced defects are conserved as detected, escaped, or censored;
- no defect is detected twice, and bypassed or non-audited work never receives
  pre-integration review detection;
- with the layer enabled, no verification-failure or requested-change draw
  uses the original combined probability, so rework is never counted twice;
- remediation never revives a terminal state or undoes an integration;
- the v0.1.0 measured quality fields stay `null` with their reason;
- every defect report renders its sensitivity ranges and the assumed basis of
  every class; and
- reports render from saved artifacts without rerunning simulation.

## Recommendation about LLM support

None, unchanged from earlier versions.

## Explicitly excluded

- source-code or AST analysis, and defect, vulnerability, or severity
  prediction from repository content;
- a measured or predicted security risk, vulnerability count, or exploit
  likelihood;
- treating requested changes, failed checks, static findings, breakages,
  reverts, or rollbacks as latent or escaped defects;
- defects introduced by fix revisions, in the first slice;
- contributor, agent, or developer quality scores or rankings;
- a claim that any review policy or size threshold is safe, appropriate, or
  recommended; and
- automatic policy selection or optimization against modeled defect outcomes.
